## 🐧 Linux: Freedom Through Understanding

![Arch Linux](https://img.shields.io/badge/CURRENT%20SYSTEM-Arch%20Linux-1793D1?style=flat-square&logo=archlinux&logoColor=white)
![Philosophy](https://img.shields.io/badge/PHILOSOPHY-Understand%20your%20tools-27272a?style=flat-square)

Linux isn't just an operating system I enjoy using. It's an ecosystem that embodies many things I value: freedom, experimentation, transparency, and the ability to make informed decisions about the tools I use.

I love the Linux community, the diversity of distributions, the documentation, the endless possibilities for customization, and the knowledge shared by people who enjoy understanding how things work.

### 🏹 Arch Linux — My Current Home

Minimalism, flexibility, and control.

Arch appeals to me because it encourages an active relationship with the operating system. Its documentation and community make it a place where curiosity can turn into practical knowledge.

I appreciate being able to shape my environment around my needs rather than treating every default as unquestionable.

### 🌀 Artix Linux — Another Way to Build a System

Artix interests me for more than its alternative init systems. It has its own identity, ecosystem, and approach to constructing a Linux environment.

I appreciate the freedom to explore different design decisions and understand how they influence the system as a whole.

### 🔥 Gentoo Linux — Understanding Through Customization

Gentoo fascinates me because of its approach to building and customizing software around specific requirements.

The relationship between compilation, configuration, dependencies, and hardware makes it an especially interesting distribution to explore.

I'm still learning, and I want to understand more of what makes it tick.

### ⚙️ OpenRC — The Machinery Behind Startup

I'm also interested in OpenRC and alternative init systems.

Understanding how a system starts, manages services, handles dependencies, and organizes its runtime environment is part of the deeper technical knowledge I want to develop.

I don't use complexity as a status symbol. I value it when it helps me understand a system more thoroughly.

And if something breaks, that's another mechanism to investigate.

## ⚖️ My Philosophy of Technology

I believe technology should make our lives easier without making us incapable of handling them ourselves.

Automation is valuable. Convenience is useful. Abstraction allows us to build things that would otherwise be impossible.

But understanding still matters.

Use automation to eliminate pointless repetition, not to abandon critical thinking. Use abstractions to manage complexity, but learn what they hide when the situation demands it.

A tool should extend your capabilities, not become a substitute for them.

The same principle applies to learning: don't worship complexity, don't fear it, and don't confuse difficulty with value.

**Learn what you use. Understand what you depend on. Question what you take for granted.**
